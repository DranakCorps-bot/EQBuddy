Five things worth knowing

  1. A measure name is not a definition. MDR (Field ASUs) is one DAX line. What it means lives four layers down, in a Teradata CASE statement inside a macro almost nobody reads.
  2. The model duplicates entities it cannot express as roles. "Field Entitlement" and "Tech Entitlement" are two physical copies of one Offering entity because DAX needs one table per relationship path. The cube cannot say they are the same thing.
  3. Two geographies compete. Asset country and service country are both modeled, from two different source databases, with no declared distinction. "MDR by region" is ambiguous and nothing in the model says so.
  4. MDR is non-additive and nothing declares it. It is a ratio of two weekly averages. Averaging it across weeks is wrong. The cube will happily let you do it.
  5. These are complements, not rivals. The Concept Layer should annotate and point at the existing Julius measures, not replace them. The cube stays the calculation engine.
Observed · the smoking gun Dim - Field Entitlement and Dim - Tech Entitlement are built from byte-identical SQL against the same two source objects. They differ only by a column-name prefix. A third copy of the same grain exists as Dim - Monarch Ranking. One real-world concept, three physical tables, zero declared equivalence.
Section 02 · Orientation

What MDR actually is

MDR identifies service events that incur warranty cost — in practice, a dispatched work order where Dell bears the parts and/or labour cost under an entitlement. On its own the count is meaningless: a bigger installed base produces more dispatches. So MDR is almost always consumed as a rate: dispatches divided by the population of assets that were eligible and entitled to generate one.

"MDR" is not one number — it is a family

The cube exposes at least ten distinct MDR variants. A human analyst picks the right one from context. An AI agent asked "what is our MDR rate?" has no basis to choose, and the model gives it no help.

Section 03 · Orientation

Two ways to describe the same thing

A semantic layer is an execution contract. It guarantees that if you ask for MDR (Field ASUs) filtered to a region, you get one deterministic, governed number. A Concept Layer is a meaning contract. It guarantees you know what that number denotes, where its definition came from, and what you are not allowed to do with it.

Semantic layer — what it encodes

  • Physical tables, columns, data types
  • Join paths, cardinality, filter direction
  • One canonical aggregation formula per measure
  • Display folders and format strings
  • Perspectives (curated table subsets)
  • Row-level security boundaries

Concept Layer — what it adds

  • Entities and roles, distinct from tables
  • Human definitions with authorship and effective dates
  • Provenance: which macro derived this flag
  • Additivity and comparability constraints
  • Synonyms, aliases, deprecations
  • Explicit statements of what is not valid

Capability comparison

The point Read the last row alongside the first. Neither column is winning. The Concept Layer cannot compute and should never try; the semantic layer cannot explain and will never be able to. The interesting architecture is the one where an agent consults the Concept Layer to plan a question and the cube to answer it.

Deep dive: three inconsistencies visible in the extract Engineers

These are things I noticed while reading the extract. I am flagging them as worth verifying against the live model, not asserting they are bugs — the catalog snapshot dates from 2026-08-06 and there may be intent I cannot see.

1. Inconsistent SUMMARIZE anchor table

-- Weekly Avg MDR summarises the DIMENSION
var tbl = SUMMARIZE('Dim - Fiscal Calendar','Dim - Fiscal Calendar'[Fiscal Week],"MDR_CNT",[MDR Work Orders])

-- Weekly Avg Field ASU summarises the FACT
var tbl = SUMMARIZE('FACT - ASU','Dim - Fiscal Calendar'[Fiscal Week],"ASUs",[Weekly Field ASUs (ALL)])

The numerator averages over every fiscal week in the calendar; the denominator averages only over weeks present in the ASU fact. In a period with calendar weeks but no ASU rows, the two averages have different divisors — and the ratio drifts.

2. TLSU exclusion flag used in opposite directions

-- Total Tech TLSUs (ALL)
CALCULATE(SUM('FACT - ASU'[tech_asus]),'Dim - Fiscal Calendar'[ASU Actual Decline] <> "DECLINE",
  'FACT - ASU'[TLA_EXCLSN_FLG]=1,'Dim - Product'[Reporting Product Type] in {BLANK(), "HARDWARE"})

-- Weekly Tech TLSUs / Weekly Field TLSUs
CALCULATE([Weekly Tech ASUs (ALL)],'FACT - ASU'[TLA_EXCLSN_FLG]<>1)

One filters = 1, the others <> 1. These are complementary populations sharing the label "TLSU". Note also that the first omits "NULL" from its product filter while every ASU measure includes it.

3. Two measures, identical body

Total Work Orders and distinct work orders have the same DAX expression. Only the first is referenced downstream. An agent doing semantic search over measure names has two equally-good hits and no tiebreaker.

Why this matters for the argument None of these are DAX failures. They are knowledge failures — drift between what a name promises and what a formula does. A semantic layer has no place to record intent, so it cannot detect divergence from it. A Concept Layer with a declared TLSU definition would surface all three as constraint violations at authoring time.
Section 05 · What we actually built

The warehouse beneath it

The DAX in section 04 consumes flags it does not define. To find out what MDR Dispatch Flag or fld_asus actually mean, you have to descend three more layers. Observed

Where MDR is really derived

03_BLUEBK_M_WO_EXT.sql builds SVC_TYPE_NEW through a long CASE, then uses it to derive the MDR flags. This is the actual business definition of "a service event that incurs warranty cost": 

/* ---- SVC_TYPE_NEW (abridged) ---- */
CASE
  WHEN wof.SVC_TYPE IN ('Parts and Labor','Labor Only')            THEN wof.SVC_TYPE
  WHEN wof.SVC_TYPE IN ('Mail-In','Collect and Return','Return to Base') THEN 'Depot'
  WHEN wof.SVC_TYPE = 'Carry-In'                                 THEN 'Parts and Labor'
  WHEN wof.SVC_TYPE = 'Parts Only' AND mdr.MDR_LBR_FLG = 1      THEN 'Tech Direct Remit'
  WHEN wof.SVC_TYPE = 'Parts Only'
       AND (mdr.MDR_LBR_FLG = 0 OR mdr.MDR_LBR_FLG IS NULL)     THEN wof.SVC_TYPE
  WHEN wof.SVC_TYPE NOT IN ('Parts Only','Parts and Labor','Labor Only')
       THEN CASE
              WHEN mdr.LBR_COST_FLG = 1 AND mdr.SHIP_COST_FLG = 1 THEN 'Parts and Labor'
              WHEN mdr.LBR_COST_FLG = 1 AND mdr.SHIP_COST_FLG = 0 THEN 'Labor Only'
              WHEN mdr.LBR_COST_FLG = 0 AND mdr.SHIP_COST_FLG = 1 THEN 'Parts Only'
              WHEN mdr.LBR_COST_FLG = 0 AND mdr.SHIP_COST_FLG = 0 THEN 'No Parts No Labor'
            END
  ELSE 'NA'
END AS SVC_TYPE_NEW

/* ---- MDR flags ---- */
CASE WHEN SVC_TYPE_NEW IN ('Parts and Labor','Parts Only','Tech Direct Remit')
          AND mdr.MDR_DSPCH_FLG = 1
     THEN 1 ELSE 0 END AS PARTS_MDR_FLG

CASE WHEN SVC_TYPE_NEW IN ('Parts and Labor','Parts Only','Tech Direct Remit','Labor Only')
          AND mdr.MDR_DSPCH_FLG = 1
          AND (wof.WO_TYPE IN ('Break Fix') OR wof.WO_TYPE IS NULL)
          AND (FQM.FQM_FLG = 0 OR FQM.FQM_FLG IS NULL)
     THEN 1 ELSE 0 END AS BREAKFIX_MDR_FLG

Observed · the grain of an ASU An ASU is a distinct SRC_CUST_PROD_ID per fiscal week, excluded if ASU_XCLSN_FLG = 1, and required to carry FLD_FLG = 1 for the field variant. That single sentence is the definition of the denominator of a KPI reported to leadership, and it exists nowhere except in a WHERE clause and in the heads of about four people.
Deep dive: how entitlement is resolved, including the sentinel values Engineers

The same macro resolves entitlement onto the work order, and encodes two important business states as magic strings:

CASE WHEN COALESCE(inc_fld.ENTLT_PROD_OFFRG_CD, inc_tech.ENTLT_PROD_OFFRG_CD) IS NULL
     THEN 1 ELSE 0 END AS NULL_POC_FLG

CASE
   WHEN NULL_ASST_FLG = 1 AND NULL_POC_FLG = 1 THEN 'N/A'   -- no asset AND no entitlement
   WHEN NULL_ASST_FLG = 0 AND NULL_POC_FLG = 1 THEN 'OOW'   -- known asset, out of warranty
   ELSE inc_fld.ENTLT_PROD_OFFRG_CD
END AS FLD_POC

'OOW' and 'N/A' flow all the way up into Dim - Field Entitlement[Field Product Offering Code] as if they were ordinary offering codes. They are not — they are absence markers with completely different semantics. Downstream, Dim - Field Entitlement has its own special-case for one of them:

CASE
  WHEN POH.PROD_OFRNG_CODE = 'OOW'                              THEN 'OOW'
  WHEN UDT.TECHL_SUPP_GRP_DESC LIKE '%ProSupport Plus%'        THEN 'ProSupport Plus'
  WHEN UDT.TECHL_SUPP_TIER_NBR IN (1,2)                        THEN 'ProSupport'
  WHEN UDT.TECHL_SUPP_TIER_NBR IN (3,4,5)                      THEN 'Basic'
  ELSE 'NA'
END AS "Offer Level"

source: data-catalog/tabular/Julius/tables/Dim - Field Entitlement.md

So we now have 'OOW', 'N/A' and 'NA' circulating as three different absence markers with overlapping meaning. An agent grouping MDR by Offer Level will produce buckets it cannot interpret, and will not know that 'OOW' is a legitimate business population (out-of-warranty service) while 'N/A' is a data quality artifact (unresolvable asset).

Scope boundary MDR_DSPCH_FLG, MDR_QUALIFY_FLG and MDR_LBR_FLG originate upstream in SVC_CDL_T.USDM_WO_MDR_FACT, surfaced through a passthrough view. Bluebook consumes them; it does not define them. I have described their consumption precisely and am deliberately not speculating about their upstream derivation.

Genuinely conformed — numerator and denominator both join on the same key to the same table, so MDR-by-product is safe. The dimension sources from LAB_SVC_USDM.USDM_PROD_HIER_VW and carries 52 columns.

The problem: hierarchy exists in the data but not in the model

Dim - Product has an obvious product hierarchy sitting in it as flat columns. The model declares no hierarchies at all — the levels are implicit:

Global rollup

GBL_LOB_NM → GBL_BRAND_NM → GBL_PROD_NM → GBL_GNRTN_NM

Item rollup

FMLY_GRND_PRNT_NM → FM_FMLY → FMLY → PROD_LN_NM → ITM_NBR

Service rollup

SVC_PROD_BU_NM → SVC_PROD_LOB_GRP_NM → SVC_PROD_GRP_NM

Three parallel, partially-overlapping rollups. An agent asked for "MDR by product line" has at least six plausible columns (PROD_LN_NM, PROD_LN, PROD_LN_CD, RPTG_LOB_NM, LOB_DESC, LOB_DESC_MODERN) and no way to rank them. There is even a LOB_REBRAND column implying a renaming event with no recorded effective date.

Observed · the bridge-key workaround Because forecast tables key on a different grain, the model synthesises a join key in DAX: 
trim(upper(COMBINEVALUES("|",'Dim - Product'[Global LOB Name],'Dim - Product'[Product Business Unit Type])))

A composite key built by string concatenation, then joined BothDirections. It works, but it encodes a real relationship ("a forecast series applies to an LOB within a business unit") as a delimiter and a trim. The relationship itself is not stated anywhere.

And the filter nobody sees Remember from section 04 that every ASU measure silently filters 'Dim - Product'[Reporting Product Type] in {BLANK(),"NULL","HARDWARE"}. So the MDR denominator is hardware-only. The numerator has no such restriction. If someone slices MDR by a software product, the denominator collapses and the rate explodes. Nothing warns them.
Section 07 · Dimension deep-dive

Geography — two competing truths

Here the model contains a genuine ambiguity that it cannot express, let alone resolve. Observed

Dim - Geo Location

Source: LAB_SVC_USDM.USDM_GEO_HIER_SRT

Joined from: FACT - ASU[ASST_UNIFIED_ISO_CTRY_CD], FACT - Work Order Parts[ASST_UNIFIED_ISO_CTRY_CD], FACT - Case[ASST_UNIFIED_ISO_CTRY_CD], and all four Forecast tables via iso_alpha2_cd

Dim - Service Location

Source: SVC_CDL.USDM_GEO_HIER_SRT
13 columns — no GSD columns

Same table name. Two different databases. Near-identical column lists. Both keyed on ISO 2 Code. Both offer Region Name, Dell Territory, Dell Area.

Why this breaks MDR specifically

The MDR numerator can reach both geographies. The denominator (FACT - ASU) can only reach Dim - Geo Location — there is no ASU relationship to Dim - Service Location at all.

The failure mode Slice MDR (Field ASUs) by Dim - Service Location[Region Name] and you get a numerator that respects the filter and a denominator that ignores it entirely. Every region returns the same global ASU count. The rate is nonsense in every cell, and it renders without error, correctly formatted as a percentage.

This is the single clearest argument in the document. It is not a bug in anyone's DAX. It is a modelling truth that has no expressible form in a tabular model: "these two dimensions are different roles of the same concept, and only one of them is valid as a denominator context."

The only difference is that the tabular layer renames every column with a Field  or Tech  prefix. And Dim - Monarch Ranking is a third table over SVC_CDL.USDM_MONARCH_RNKG_UDT_DIM — one of the two sources the other two already join to.

Why the duplication is not incompetence This is the correct thing to do in a tabular model. DAX resolves filter context through physical relationships; a single table cannot serve two simultaneously-active filter paths from one fact. Duplicating the dimension is the standard, recommended pattern for role-playing dimensions. The point is not that the modeller got it wrong — it is that the paradigm forces a lossy encoding, and the lost information is exactly the information an agent needs.

What gets lost

  • Equivalence. Nothing states that Field Product Offering Code and Tech Product Offering Code draw from the same code set. An agent joining MDR by one and ASU by the other will silently produce a cross-product of two identical domains.
  • Duplicated derivation. The SUPPORT_TIER and Offer Level CASE logic is copy-pasted into both dimension queries. Two copies, one intent, no link between them.
  • Sentinel semantics. As shown in section 05, 'OOW' and 'N/A' arrive as offering codes. Only one of the three dimensions special-cases 'OOW'.
  • Tier ordering. TECHL_SUPP_TIER_NBR 1–5 is an ordinal scale where 1 is the richest entitlement. Modeled as Int64, so a chart sorts it ascending and shows the best coverage first with no indication that this is a rank, not a quantity.
Section 09 · The concept layer

The same concept, as a Concept Layer

Everything from here on is an architectural proposal, not something that exists. Proposed The design goal is deliberately modest: describe what the Julius model already computes, add the facts the model cannot hold, and point at the existing DAX rather than reimplementing it.

Four things the Concept Layer adds

1. Roles instead of duplicate tables

One ServiceOffering entity with FieldRole, TechRole, RankingRole. One Country entity with AssetCountryRole and ServiceCountryRole. The three physical dimension tables become realisations of roles, not separate concepts.

2. Definitions with provenance

"Eligible entitled asset" gets a prose definition, the literal predicate ASU_XCLSN_FLG <> 1 AND FLD_FLG = 1, and a prov:wasDerivedFrom link to the macro file that owns it. Change the macro, the link goes stale, and drift becomes detectable.

3. Constraints as data

"MDR Rate is non-additive across weeks." "MDR Rate may only be dimensioned by the asset-country role." "The denominator is hardware-only." Each is a node an agent can retrieve before it composes a query, not a footnote it will never read.

4. A typed measure family

The ten MDR variants from section 02 become instances with declared restrictions and units, so "which MDR do I want?" is resolvable by traversal instead of by string similarity against a display folder name.

Design constraint I would insist on The Concept Layer holds no numbers and no calculation logic. Its bb:implementedBy edge is a pointer to the Julius measure, and that measure remains the only place a figure is ever produced. The moment a Concept Layer starts recomputing KPIs, you have built a second, unreconciled semantic layer — which is strictly worse than having one.
Section 10 · The concept layer

Three serializations, side by side

The same MDR concept, expressed three ways of writing a Concept Layer down. These are directly comparable — identical entities, identical constraints, identical provenance — so the differences you see are purely methodological. The first uses ontology techniques (RDF/OWL) specifically, since that is the most formally rigorous way to build one. Proposed 

@prefix bb:   <https://bluebook.internal/ontology#> .
@prefix rdfs: <http://www.w3.org/2000/01/rdf-schema#> .
@prefix owl:  <http://www.w3.org/2002/07/owl#> .
@prefix skos: <http://www.w3.org/2004/02/skos/core#> .
@prefix prov: <http://www.w3.org/ns/prov#> .

### ---------- The event we are counting ----------
bb:WarrantyCostEvent a owl:Class ;
    rdfs:subClassOf  bb:ServiceEvent ;
    skos:prefLabel   "MDR Dispatch"@en ;
    skos:altLabel    "MDR Work Order"@en , "Warranty-Incurring Dispatch"@en ;
    skos:definition  """A dispatched work order for which Dell incurs parts and/or
                        labour cost under a customer entitlement."""@en ;
    bb:physicalFlag  "FACT - Work Order Parts[MDR Dispatch Flag]" ;
    prov:wasDerivedFrom <macro:03_BLUEBK_M_WO_EXT.sql#PARTS_MDR_FLG> .

### ---------- The population we divide by ----------
bb:EligibleEntitledAsset a owl:Class ;
    skos:prefLabel   "Active Serviced Unit"@en ;
    skos:altLabel    "ASU"@en , "Eligible Entitled Asset"@en ;
    skos:definition  """A distinct customer product, counted once per fiscal week,
                        that is not ASU-excluded and carries the delivery-arm flag."""@en ;

  5. Respect additivity. bb:additivity = "non-additive" plus the NoCrossWeekAverage constraint stops it from averaging the ratio. It recomputes from base counts.

The honest case against doing this

A wrong Concept Layer is worse than no Concept Layer If bb:validGeographyRole says AssetCountryRole and someone later adds an ASU relationship to Dim - Service Location, the Concept Layer now actively prevents a valid query while projecting total confidence. The semantic layer at least fails honestly — it just doesn't know. A curated layer that has silently rotted is a liability, and the failure is harder to detect than the one it was built to prevent.
  • It is a standing commitment, not a project. Concept definitions need an owner and a review cadence forever. Without that, budget for 18 months of value and then decay.
  • Coverage is all-or-nothing in practice. An agent that finds Concept Layer context for six of ten concepts will silently fall back to guessing on the other four, and the user cannot tell which mode produced which answer.
  • Much of the benefit is achievable more cheaply. Adding descriptions to the 30 measures people actually use, declaring the Product hierarchies in the model, and renaming Dim - Service Location to something unambiguous would capture a meaningful share of the value for a fraction of the effort. That should be tried first.
  • Nothing here fixes upstream. MDR_DSPCH_FLG is still defined in a system we do not own. The Concept Layer can record that fact — which is useful — but it cannot make the definition visible.
Where I actually land The case is strongest not for "MDR%" in general but for the specific failure in section 07 — a query that returns a well-formatted, plausible, wrong number with no error. That class of failure scales badly with agent adoption, because agents will run these queries far more often than humans ever did, and with far less suspicion. That is the argument worth making, and it is narrower and more defensible than "we should build a Concept Layer."

Section 15 · Judgement

Deriving it from evidence

Section 12's strongest objection to this whole idea was authoring burden and silent rot. The most promising answer is to stop hand-authoring definitions and start harvesting them from artifacts the organisation already produces. Proposed

Assessment of the approach

The proposal — capture example reports and scorecards alongside meeting transcripts and query statistics from the cubes and Teradata DBC tables — is, I think, correct in its core intuition, and it decomposes the problem in exactly the right way:

Each source answers a different question

  • System metadata tells you what is possible.
  • Query logs tell you what is practised.
  • Reports and scorecards tell you what is ratified.
  • Transcripts tell you what was intended.

Why that combination works

No single source yields meaning. Structure without convention gives you 8 defensible answers to "MDR by region". Convention without intent tells you what people do but not whether it is right. Intent without structure cannot be executed. The union is genuinely more than the parts — and critically, three of the four sources already exist and cost nothing to start collecting.

The failure mode this must be designed around Frequency is not validity. If MDR has been sliced by Dim - Service Location four hundred times over two years, usage mining will confidently conclude that this is the canonical pattern — and encode the precise bug the Concept Layer exists to prevent, now carrying the authority of evidence. That is strictly worse than the current situation, because a hand-authored mistake looks like an opinion whereas a mined mistake looks like a fact.

The design consequence is non-negotiable: harvesting produces candidates, never assertions. Every derived statement carries an evidence class, and nothing is enforceable until a human has promoted it.

A second, subtler problem: survivorship bias. Query logs only contain questions someone knew how to ask and had the skill to express in DAX or SQL. The questions that were abandoned, routed to a human, or never attempted leave no trace — and that silent population is precisely the one an agent is meant to serve. Mining usage optimises the Concept Layer for yesterday's analysts. Worth supplementing with a deliberate capture of failed and escalated questions.

Evidence sources, ranked by what they are worth

I would reorder the priority slightly from the proposal. Published scorecards are the single highest-value artifact and tend to be under-weighted, because a published report is not just usage — it is a definition somebody signed their name to. Reports & scorecards — the highest-value source Start here

Capture the report definition, not a screenshot or an export. From PBIP/TMDL or the report JSON:

  • Visual → measure mapping. Which of the 203 measures are actually on a published page. This alone collapses the scope problem: build the Concept Layer for those first.
  • Filter stack at all three levels — visual, page, and report. Report-level filters are where unstated scope assumptions hide (the hardware-only restriction from section 06 is exactly this shape).
  • Slicer defaults and sort order. A default is a convention nobody wrote down.
  • Footnotes, subtitles and tooltip text. Genuinely the richest vein in the whole exercise — this is where analysts put the caveats that could not be expressed in the model. "Excludes OOW", "13-week rolling", "hardware only" all live here.
  • Audience and distribution. Distinguishes the exec-facing definition from the seventeen ad-hoc variants.
  • Refresh cadence and as-of convention. Feeds the grain and latency properties.

Cube query statistics — convention mining Engineers

  • $SYSTEM.DISCOVER_OBJECT_ACTIVITY — per-object read counters. A cheap periodic snapshot, no tracing infrastructure required. Good first move if xEvents needs approval.

What not to do with it: promote any pairing to a rule without step 6 of the derivation table.

Meeting transcripts — the only source of intent Highest noise, highest uniqueness

Noisy and effortful to extract, and worth it anyway, because nothing else in the estate records why. To make them usable:

  • Separate decision from discussion. A transcript is mostly exploration. Extract only resolved statements, and mark them as such.
  • Keep attribution and date. "Someone said X" is not evidence; "[SME] said X on 2026-05-14" is, and it lets you resolve conflicts by recency and authority.
  • Capture disagreements explicitly. A contested definition is a risk marker worth surfacing to an agent as low confidence, not something to flatten into a single answer.
  • Link to the action. The @meeting-notes-issue-sync skill already turns transcripts into GitLab issues — that link is the bridge from "we decided" to "it was implemented", and it is what makes the rationale verifiable.
  • Quote verbatim, never paraphrase. A paraphrased rationale is a new assertion with a false provenance claim attached.

Two suggestions on sequencing

1. Do not build the harvester first Derive the MDR concept document by hand from these five sources, for one concept, and keep notes on which source actually earned its keep. That takes days. Building the harvesting pipeline first tells you what is easy to harvest, which is emphatically not the same as what is worth harvesting — and query logs are easy while scorecard footnotes are hard, so automation-first will systematically over-collect the least valuable source.
2. Define the success metric before collecting anything Something falsifiable. For example: assemble twenty held-out questions from the transcript corpus, have an agent answer each with and without Concept Layer context, and have an SME score correctness and whether appropriate caveats were stated. If the Concept Layer arm does not clearly win, the finding is genuinely useful and the cost stops there. Without a pre-committed metric this becomes a project that can never be concluded, only abandoned.

One caveat on that metric: score caveat quality separately from numeric correctness. The failure this whole document is about — section 07 — produces a wrong number that looks right. A scoring rubric that only checks the figure will miss precisely the improvement being tested.

Glossary

Terms in the body text carry dotted underlines — hover or tab to them for a definition. Everything is collected here as well, for printing and for anyone who prefers a list.

This document's core term
Concept Layer

This document's term for a business-meaning layer that sits above the semantic layer: definitions, relationships, constraints and provenance for the concepts a cube calculates, addressable by an agent before it queries. In AI and knowledge-management literature this is usually called an ontology — the two terms describe the same thing. This document uses "Concept Layer" throughout and reserves "ontology" for the specific W3C technology family (RDF/OWL) shown in section 10.

Knowledge representation & the RDF/OWL family
Ontology

The academic and AI-industry name for what this document calls a Concept Layer: a formal, machine-readable model of what exists in a domain, what it's called, how it relates, and what rules constrain it. Broader than a data model — it describes meaning rather than storage.

RDF

Resource Description Framework. A W3C standard for describing anything as three-part statements — subject, predicate, object — called triples. One of three ways this document shows a Concept Layer being written down.

Triple

The atomic unit of RDF. "MDR Rate" / "has denominator" / "ASU" is one triple. A Concept Layer built this way is just a large pile of these.

OWL

Web Ontology Language. A W3C standard layered on RDF that adds classes, properties and logical axioms, so software can infer new facts rather than only retrieve stated ones.

Turtle (.ttl)

Terse RDF Triple Language. A compact, human-readable text syntax for writing RDF. The format shown in the first serialization tab.

SPARQL

The query language for RDF, roughly what SQL is to relational tables. Pronounced "sparkle".

JSON-LD

JSON for Linked Data. Ordinary JSON plus an @context block mapping its keys to shared global identifiers — readable by any JSON parser, and simultaneously valid RDF. The most practical way to hand a Concept Layer to an LLM agent.

SKOS

Simple Knowledge Organization System. A small W3C vocabulary for labels, synonyms and plain-language definitions (prefLabel, altLabel, definition). The human-readable layer of a Concept Layer written in RDF.

PROV / PROV-O

A W3C vocabulary for recording where something came from — which file, process or person derived it. Used here to link a concept back to the macro that defines it.

IRI

Internationalised Resource Identifier. A globally unique, usually URL-shaped name for a thing. It does not have to resolve to an actual web page.

Property graph

A graph database model of nodes and relationships, both able to carry arbitrary key/value properties. Less formal than RDF, often easier for path and reachability questions. Neo4j is the best-known implementation.

Cypher

The query language of Neo4j and other property-graph databases, using ASCII-art patterns such as (a)-[:REL]->(b).

BI & dimensional modelling
Semantic layer

The business-friendly modelling tier between raw tables and reports — here, the SSAS Tabular model. It defines measures, join paths and friendly names so report authors never write SQL.

DAX

Data Analysis Expressions. The formula language of SSAS Tabular, Power BI and Power Pivot. Every measure quoted in this document is DAX.

Measure

A named calculation evaluated dynamically against whatever filters the user has applied, rather than a stored value. MDR (Field ASUs) is a measure.

Grain

The level of detail one row represents. An ASU row's grain is "one asset, one fiscal week".

Conformed dimension

A dimension that means the same thing and joins the same way across several fact tables, so measures from those facts can safely be compared side by side.

Role-playing dimension

One real-world entity used in several capacities — one Country acting as both "asset country" and "service country". Tabular models normally implement this by physically duplicating the table, which is where the information loss in section 08 comes from.

Cardinality

How many rows on each side of a relationship can match. Many:One means many fact rows point at one dimension row.

Cross-filter direction

Whether a relationship propagates filters one way (dimension filters fact) or both ways. Bidirectional filtering is powerful but can create ambiguous or circular evaluation.

Additivity

Whether a measure can validly be summed or averaged across a dimension. Ratios and distinct counts are typically non-additive: they must be recomputed from base values rather than aggregated.

Sentinel value

A special value stored in an ordinary field to mean "absent" or "not applicable" — here OOW, N/A and NA. They look like data but are really status markers.

Partition

A slice of a table loaded by its own query. FACT - ASU has 17, each a separate quarter-scoped view, with its own copy of the load logic.

Platform & instrumentation
DBC

Teradata's system catalog database. Holds the data dictionary (DBC.TablesV, DBC.ColumnsV) and the query logs.

DBQL

Database Query Log. Teradata's built-in query logging, written to DBC tables. Records who ran what, when, how long it took, and which objects and columns it touched.

DMV

Dynamic Management View. A system view inside SSAS, addressed as $SYSTEM.*, reporting on model structure and runtime activity.

Extended Events

The tracing framework for SQL Server and SSAS. The QueryEnd event captures the full query text, duration and user — the raw material for usage mining.

Macro (Teradata)

A stored, parameterised set of SQL statements executed as a unit. The BLUEBK_M_* macros are where Bluebook's real business logic lives.

Power Query / M

The data-loading language used by each tabular partition to fetch rows from a source. Distinct from DAX, which computes over the data once loaded.

Bluebook domain terms
MDR

The measure family identifying service events that incur warranty cost, usually expressed as a rate over the eligible entitled asset base. The acronym expansion is not recorded in the captured metadata, so it is deliberately not guessed at here.

ASU

Active Serviced Unit. A distinct customer product counted once per fiscal week, excluded if ASU_XCLSN_FLG = 1 and required to carry the relevant delivery-arm flag. The denominator of most Bluebook rates.

TLSU

A variant of the ASU population defined by the TLA exclusion flag, used as an alternative denominator. Note the inconsistent filter direction flagged in section 04.

POC

Product Offering Code. The entitlement code attached to an asset or work order, appearing as FLD_POC and TECH_POC on the work-order fact.

OOW

Out Of Warranty. A sentinel written into the offering-code field when the asset is known but carries no entitlement. A legitimate business population, not a data error.

FQM

A work-order flag (FQM_FLG) that identifies field-quality-programme dispatches and carves them out of the standard break/fix MDR population. Expansion not recorded in the captured metadata.

RDR

Repeat Dispatch Rate. Measured over 7- and 28-day windows, in both a Legacy and a Customer variant.

Fiscal week

The reporting grain for ASU and MDR. Both operands of the MDR rate are averaged over fiscal weeks, which is what makes the result non-additive.
