## §3 Where the gate attaches to the Soft loop — and the one place it must not

### §3.1 The attach point

EQBuddy's Soft loop has exactly one plan-SIGN ask: **a LIVE ASK appended to
`HELM-FEEDBACK.md`, pushed, followed by the `helm-back-channel.yml` wake.**
That is the ask DRA-304 §3's scope line names, and it is the only place the
gate attaches.

So: **a plan that trips C1–C5 carries its `challenge:` line before its LIVE ASK
goes up.** The line is written into the plan body (`docs/plans/DRA-<n>.md`,
beside the `route:` tag) and restated in the ask. Verdict semantics, the
gate-outcome mapping, the `challenge-overrule:` line and the 6-hour NO-RETURN
rule are **DRA-304 §4's and are inherited verbatim** — this card does not
re-specify them and must not drift from them.

### §3.2 The seam that matters more than the attach point

**The gate fires ONCE, at the plan's SIGN. It never fires per slice.**

This is the single most important EQBuddy-side statement in this file, and it is
not a convenience. `docs/ops/execution-flow.md` §4 is the measurement: a signed
plan authorizes every slice it declares, in order, on green gates, because in the
DRA-70/71/72 window three Helm coverage gaps produced stalls of 3.5–6.4 h
accounting for **15.3 of 18.2 total wait-hours — 84%** — all of it planned work
parked at an authorization boundary, while **Helm's pre-merge change rate over
the same window was ~0%**.

A per-slice Challenger wake would re-create, one layer down, precisely the cost
that cutover bought out. The gate is allowed to add writing and one bounded
wait to a *plan*; it is not allowed to add a boundary to a *slice sequence*.

**The seam is the one that already exists.** A slice that exceeds the plan's
declared files, behaviour or boundary stops and escalates — and that escalation
*is* a new plan-SIGN ask, so it takes the gate at §3.1 like any other. Nothing
new is invented to catch it.

### §3.3 What does not trip the gate, stated so the default stays off

- **V0–V1 work.** It never reaches a plan-SIGN ask. The routing table in
  `CLAUDE.md` is untouched.
- **Every slice after D1 of a signed plan.** §3.2.
- **The inboxes.** `SCRIBE.md`, `BEVEL.md`, `FABLE.md` inform; they never
  trigger an unattended agent, and they do not wake the Challenger either.
- **Helm's operational doors** — a release click, a posture signature on a
  public reply, a hold. DRA-304 §3 rejected these by name: the charter scopes
  the role to *"the reasoning behind consequenceful research, strategy and
  plans,"* and a release click has no plan-reasoning to challenge. Where a
  door-touching *plan* exists, C1–C5 catch it upstream.
- **The consequence list.** Those eight are David's, and they are decided with
  the question tool. A Challenger argument does not insert itself in front of
  the Founder. Where a consequence-list item also has a plan, the plan takes the
  gate; the door still goes to David, unchanged.
- **Reviewer diffs, Sr quick-passes, rotation PRs carrying out a SIGNed rule.**
  A DRA-154 rotation executes a standing rule; it neither re-opens nor changes
  one.

## §6 Measurement, and the one thing this card owes DRA-306

**No new tag, no new instrument.** The gate is the wake-producing mechanism of
`exo-experiment: challenger-seat` (charter §8; tag on DRA-300, baseline on
DRA-301). EQBuddy contributes cards to that count; it does not open a second
ladder, which `CURRENT_ORG.md`'s one-ladder amendment forbids.

**Why §5 ships no guard.** A `challenge:`-line must-list is exactly the shape
trap 34 asks for — a guard that forbids the wrong thing cannot see a *missing*
thing, so "no plan may skip the gate" needs a curated must-list to pair with it.
It is not written here for one reason: the thing it would assert is under
challenge on DRA-306, and a guard built on a rule that may be REFRAMEd is a
guard written twice. **If DRA-304's §3 marker survives, the must-list is the
first implement card off this SPEC.** Naming that here is what stops it being
forgotten, which is trap 34's own failure mode.

### §6.1 The datum this repo owes DRA-306, question 2

DRA-306 is pressing whether the mandatory `challenge: … -> NOT-ENGAGED` line on
every card buys what it costs. **EQBuddy has measured that exact shape, and the
result cuts against the marker:**

Trap 73 — *a schema that has a field for every question becomes a licence to
ANSWER every question.* `GuideCatalog`'s six-question schema was read as a
fill-in bar, and **48 authored steps came back carrying 10 distinct values**,
nineteen of them asserting facts the cited wiki pages do not state. The defect
was found by **counting distinct values, not by reading** — Fable's #480 last
look — and the remedy was to make the fields optional and deny-list the invented
sentences.

A mandatory keyed line on every plan is that shape: a per-item field, a
low-effort default value, and no cost to filling it in. **The prediction is
therefore falsifiable and cheap:** if the `challenge:` lines across the first N
plans show a high row-count against a low distinct-count, the marker is a
template rather than a judgement, and direction B is being paid without
direction A being bought. The distinct-count telltale is already Corps-legible —
it is how trap 73 was caught — and it needs no new instrument.

This is offered to DRA-306 as **evidence, not steering**, and it argues against
a section of a SPEC I wrote.
